Difference between an iPad and a Tablet?

In Nepalese society, there is a big misconception about tablets and iPhones. Most people think these two are different devices.  But the iPad is nothing but a tablet of the popular brand iPhone.  So basically, a Tablet is a versatile, portable device with a touchscreen display that requires internet access for many of its functions. It is smaller and lighter than a laptop but larger than a smartphone, and it operates on various operating systems like Android, Windows, or iOS. An iPad, on the other hand, is a specific type of tablet developed by Apple and runs on the iOS operating system. While all iPads are tablets, not all tablets are iPads, as other tablets typically use different operating systems like Android or Windows. Some of the important differences are:

Things to consider before buying Tablets in Nepal:

Price and Budget

When purchasing a tablet, price and budget are primary considerations. Determine your spending limit and look for tablets within that range. Prices vary significantly based on brand, features, and specifications. Budget tablets offer basic functionalities suitable for everyday tasks like browsing and streaming, while mid-range tablets provide better performance, display quality, and additional features. High-end tablets come with advanced capabilities, powerful processors, high-resolution displays, and premium build quality, catering to professional and intensive users. It's essential to balance your budget with your needs to ensure you get the best value for your money without overspending.

Screen Size and Quality

The screen size and quality of a tablet significantly impact your user experience. Screen sizes typically range from 7 inches to 13 inches. Smaller screens are more portable and easier to handle, while larger screens offer a better viewing experience for media consumption, gaming, and productivity tasks. Screen quality is equally important, with higher-resolution screens providing sharper and clearer images. Look for features like IPS or AMOLED displays for better colour accuracy and viewing angles. Consider factors like brightness levels and whether the screen has anti-glare properties, especially if you'll use the tablet outdoors or in brightly lit environments.

Thickness and Weight

Thickness and weight play a crucial role in the portability and comfort of using a tablet. Thinner and lighter tablets are more convenient to carry around and hold for extended periods, making them ideal for reading, browsing, or watching videos. However, a thinner design might compromise battery life and durability. Conversely, slightly thicker and heavier tablets often have better battery life and sturdier builds. Assess your usage patterns and preferences—whether you need a highly portable device or can compromise a bit on portability for better battery and durability—before planning.

Operating System Software

The operating system (OS) software determines the user interface, app availability, and overall user experience of a tablet. The main OS options are iOS (Apple), Android, and Windows. iOS offers a smooth, secure, and optimized experience with a wide range of high-quality apps, but it is exclusive to Apple iPads. Android provides a flexible, customizable experience with a vast selection of apps and devices from various manufacturers, suiting different budgets. Windows tablets are suitable for productivity tasks, offering a desktop-like experience and compatibility with Windows software. Choose an OS that aligns with your preferences, existing ecosystem, and specific needs.

Storage Capacity

Storage capacity is a key factor, especially if you plan to store a lot of media files, apps, and documents on your tablet. Tablets typically come with storage options ranging from 16GB to 1TB. Consider your usage patterns—if you primarily stream content and use cloud storage, lower storage might suffice. For offline media consumption, gaming, and extensive app usage, higher storage is beneficial. Some tablets offer expandable storage via microSD cards, providing flexibility. Assess the internal storage and expansion options to ensure you have adequate space for your needs without constantly managing storage limitations.

Camera

The camera quality on a tablet can be important, especially if you use it for video calls, taking photos, or recording videos. While tablets generally don't match the camera quality of smartphones, higher-end models often feature better cameras with advanced functionalities. Look for specifications like megapixel count, aperture size, and additional features like autofocus, image stabilization, and flash. Front-facing cameras are crucial for video calls, so ensure they meet your requirements. If photography is a priority, consider a tablet with a high-quality rear camera, but for most users, a decent front camera suffices for everyday use.

Hardware Configuration

Hardware configuration, including the processor, RAM, and graphics, affects a tablet’s performance. A powerful processor ensures smooth multitasking, fast app loading, and efficient handling of demanding tasks. More RAM enhances the tablet’s ability to manage multiple applications simultaneously without slowing down. For gaming and media editing, a robust graphics processor is crucial. Evaluate the specifications to match your usage—basic tasks require less powerful hardware, while professional work, gaming, and heavy multitasking benefit from higher-end configurations. Understanding the hardware components helps in selecting a tablet that delivers the required performance and longevity.

Extra Features

Extra features can enhance the functionality and user experience of a tablet. Consider features like stylus support, which is beneficial for note-taking, drawing, and design work. Keyboard compatibility transforms a tablet into a productive workstation, especially for those who need to type extensively. Look for features like fast charging, biometric security (fingerprint or facial recognition), and multi-window capabilities for improved efficiency. Connectivity options, including USB-C, HDMI, and headphone jacks, add to the versatility. Assess the additional features that align with your specific needs and preferences to maximize the utility of your tablet.
